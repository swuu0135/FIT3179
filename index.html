Australia's Nature-Based Tourism

Introduction

Australia is wildly known as a popular place for tourism. Every year there are millions of domestic and international trips across states. These trips can be categorised based on whether it is international or domestic and also whether it is a business trip or simply just for holidays. Nature-based tourism is part of these numbers of trips made by people as Australia is known for its unique wildlife and ancient landscapes , but before we look future into that topic let's have a look at the general statistics on tourism in Australia.

2. What are the nature based activities in Australia?

So what do we mean when we say nature based activities in Australia? Australia is globally renowned for its vast, pristine nature and its commitment to preserving unique ecosystems that shelter some of the world's rarest wildlife. When we say nature based activities in Australia, we are referring to, but not limited to birdwatching, bushwalking, going to beaches, botancial gardens, and so on.

Whale and dolphin watching on the other hand is more common around the outer areas of Australia, which makes sense as it is closer to the ocean (the water) where these creatures live.

Source: Great Barrier Reef Marine Park Authority

To examine specific regional impacts on nature-based tourism, the line chart illustrates the total visitor volume to the Great Barrier Reef from 2017 through recent years. The data clearly captures a dramatic, sharp collapse in visitation around late 2020 and early 2021, where absolute numbers dropped to historic lows due to severe pandemic border closures and lockdowns. Following this downturn, the region shows a resilent recovery with seasonal peaks steadily climbing back toward a baseline of roughly 500,000 to 700,000 quarterly visits.

Source: Regional Tourism Satellite Account

Shifting focus to regional spending habits, the bar chart details tourism product consumption along the Australia Coral Coast for the 2023-24 financial period. The data reveals that long-distance passenger transportation, takeaway and restaurant meals, and accommodation services constitute the top three largest tourism expenses in the region, with transport leading at nearly 300 value units. Conversely, specialised sectors such as education, gambling, and local area transportation receive minimal direct financial intake, highlighting that regional tourism revenue is overwhelmingly driven by essential transit, dining, and lodging infrastructure.

Conclusion

In conclusion, nature-based activities serve as a foundational pillar for both regional and national tourism in Australia. The data across this dashboard clearly demonstrates that while raw visitor volumes are heavily concentrated in major states like New South Wales and Victoria, smaller regions like Tasmania rely far more critically on tourism for their local economic output. Furthermore, regional hotspots like the Great Barrier Reef and the Coral Coast show resilient post-pandemic recoveries, driven overwhelmingly by essential transit, domestic dining, and lodging infrastructure.
